Dear Peter,

a short question.  For pi0 electroproduction, at fixed W and t, does theory
constrain R = sigma_L/sigma_T in the limit Q^2 -> 0?

The naive argument says it must vanish: the longitudinal polarisation vector
becomes proportional to q at the real photon point, so current conservation
gives M_L ~ sqrt(Q^2) and hence sigma_L ~ Q^2, while sigma_T goes to the
photoproduction cross section.  That would give R ~ Q^2 -> 0.  Is that correct,
and is it exact?

I ask because the leading-twist amplitude ~ 1/Q gives sigma_L ~ 1/Q^2 instead,
so any model built on it has R growing as Q^2 falls.  If the limit is as above,
is there a standard way to impose it — a factor Q^2/(Q^2 + m^2), say — and what
scale m would you use?
